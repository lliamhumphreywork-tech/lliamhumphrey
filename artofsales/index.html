Lliam Humphrey, 20, Johannesburg

I was working in a hotel when I figured this out.

It was my gap year. Every day I watched managers in their 40s doing the exact same job I was doing. I decided that couldn't be me.

So I started posting online. 100 days straight, no audience, no results. Then one honest video about my gap year hit 500K views, and the people who reached out after it got me my first sales role.

That role was appointment setting: getting on calls with people who were interested, finding out what they needed, and booking them in with a closer. I didn't need a degree or experience. I needed to learn how to have the conversation.

"Since March I've set the calls behind $200K+ in cash collected, working remotely as an appointment setter. I booked the calls, I didn't close them. I made six figures in rand doing it."

Most people my age who are stuck where I was never get shown the door into remote sales. Not because they can't do it, but because nobody explains how it actually works. Art of Sales is that door. It's everything I wish someone had shown me back in that hotel.

None of that means you're doing something wrong. It means you need a skill people will pay for that doesn't need a degree or years of experience, even if you've never sold anything. That's what setting is.

Not for you if you want to get rich quick, or if you won't put in the work.

Why setting is the way in

You don't need experience

Setting is conversations, not hard selling. You find out what someone needs and book them in with a closer.

Teams hire remotely

Businesses selling high-ticket offers need setters to book calls for their closers. Most don't care where you live.

It's a skill you keep

Once you can run a good call, it works for any offer. And it's the first step to closing.

The path

  1. Learn the setter skill

    Work through the training and the scripts at your own pace.

  2. Practise live

    Role-play calls in the weekly sessions until they feel normal.

  3. Apply for setter roles

    Use what you've practised to land your first remote role.

  4. Move up to closing

    Once you're booking calls well, learn to close them.

Questions

Do I need sales experience?

No. The training starts from zero. If you can hold a normal conversation, you can learn this.

How much time does it take each week?

Plan for 4 to 6 hours of practice a week, plus the live session on Thursday evenings (South African time). You can do it around your job.

Is this a job?

No. Art of Sales is training and a community. It doesn't employ you or pay you. It gets you ready to apply for remote sales roles.

Can I cancel?

Yes, anytime. You pay month to month.

What if I don't get hired?

There's no guarantee you will. Most first roles are commission-only, and it can take a while. The skills stay with you either way, and you can keep practising with us.
