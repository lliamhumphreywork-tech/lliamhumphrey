The problem

The people who should know you don't, and the ones who do can't see what you know.

You have decades of judgement. Clients trust you in a room. Online you have a profile that was last touched years ago, or a post every few months that someone on your team wrote in a hurry.

Meanwhile, the people who decide who to call, who to refer and who to invite on stage are scrolling LinkedIn. They see whoever shows up every week.

You know you should be posting. You don't have the time, and you don't want to sound like everyone else.

What happened last time

I ran the LinkedIn presence for a boutique investment banking firm. We built an engine that pulled real market news relevant to their clients and strategy, drafted the posts, and I reviewed them weekly with the CEO's input.

A few weeks in, the CEO told me a Wells Fargo contact had reached out to him directly. Later he said we could pause posting for now.

That is the kind of result I'm after. I can't promise you a specific deal, and nobody honest can. I can promise you will show up every week with something worth reading, and the people who matter will notice.
